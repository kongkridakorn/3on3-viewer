    Mechanisms
    3, one per lid

    Each lid has its own wide push bar, spring gear and hinge pin. How it works: the bar is a rocker on a pivot peg. Two hook arms at its ends sit above small catch ribs inside the lid front wall, so the lid cannot rise. Press the bar about 1 mm and the hooks slide off the ribs, then the spring gear lifts the lid. A small compression spring in the front wall behind the bar pushes it back (drawn as a plain cylinder). Lid printed without catch ribs? Print the clip-on fangs (plate 7, 8 pieces, 7.6 wide x 6.4 tall x 1.7 mm thick, lying on the large flat face). Glue one to the inside of the lid front wall at each end of the push-bar window: inner end flush with the window end, bottom flush with the wall bottom edge (top face 6.4 mm above it), flat face against the wall, so the fang points into the lid like the catch ribs in this model. Lid gear: each lid now has one more full tooth at the end of the gear sector (tip at -70 deg about the hinge), so the spring gear stays in mesh to the end of the opening swing (tooth pair in mesh up to 92 deg). The back face of the gear zone has an extra 0.95 mm of material (y 29.0 to 29.95, inside the tooth root circle) so the thin wall beside the hinge hole is stronger. Catch: the push bar is your latest button_1_print.stl, unchanged (original hook arm, no notch). With it the lid ribs can only be 2.3 mm deep (y -37.1 to -34.8): a deeper rib stays under the arm and the lid never frees. The rib top is an arc concentric with the bar pivot, with two grooves. Simulated release needs about 8.25 of the 12.75 deg press travel, margin 2 deg before the bar hits the casing. New lids and this push bar are a pair.
